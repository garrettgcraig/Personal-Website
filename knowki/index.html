KnowKi

Learn the birds, mammals, plants, insects, herps, fish and mollusks around you by name, through short photo lessons built from real iNaturalist sightings. Free, with no ads and no account.

Played a KnowKi Daily?

Every day, everyone gets the same five species, easy to hard, in a U.S. and a World edition. You have 15 seconds per photo, and faster right answers score more, up to 100 each. The squares in a shared result show how each round went: green for 70 points or more, yellow for 40 or more, orange for any points, and red for a miss. Get KnowKi on the App Store to play today's.

Get to know ki

Robin Wall Kimmerer, a Potawatomi botanist and writer, suggests ki (pronounced like “key”) for any living being of the Earth, in place of “it”: a bird, a tree, a snail is someone, not something. Together they are kin, in place of “them.” KnowKi helps you get to know the kin around you by name, until you can recognize kin in the wild with no key.

Your guide is Āki, a kiwi from Aotearoa New Zealand. In te reo Māori, āki means to encourage, urge on and challenge, and that’s Āki’s job: to be at your side on your learning journey. The name also echoes the start of the Anishinaabe Aakibmaadiziiwin, “a being of the earth,” from which Kimmerer drew ki (Orion, “Speaking of Nature”).

Why it matters

“In the end we will conserve only what we love; we will love only what we understand; and we will understand only what we are taught.” (Baba Dioum, 1968). Every species you learn to recognize is one more neighbor you'll notice, care about and speak up for.

How it works

  • Learn: choose a place, a radius and a group. Your path introduces six species at a time: meet them with a few photos, practice them, then pass a checkpoint. Lessons are ten photos with four choices, drawn from research-grade iNaturalist observations recorded near your place, most commonly recorded species first.
  • Trips: learn the kin of anywhere else, from any country to every U.S. national park and E.O. Wilson's Key Places, the richest places for life on Earth, which he named after asking naturalists around the world. Each trip has its own path, and your home path stays as it is.
  • Play: the Daily gives everyone the same five species, easy to hard, in a U.S. and a World edition. Faster answers score more; share your squares when you're done. Free practice offers quick quizzes and a matching game outside the path.
  • Fieldbook: every species you've seen, with your progress, a short introduction from Wikipedia, and a map of where it's found.

Missed species come back in your next session, and species you get right come back after 1, 3 and 7 days. A species becomes kin once you recognize it in two different photos on two different days.

Go meet your kin

Once you have 10 kin, KnowKi suggests which of them are out and about near you this month, so you can go find them. When you do, photograph ki and post it to iNaturalist: once others confirm it, your sighting helps scientists, and it could become a photo in KnowKi. Connect your iNaturalist username in Settings to collect a stamp in your Fieldbook for each species you've found.

Common questions

A photo is too blurry or small to identify from.

In a lesson, tap Try a different photo before you answer to see another photo of the same species. The choices stay the same, so it doesn't give the answer away. In the Daily, Different photo swaps once per round for a small point penalty. If a photo seems wrong or inappropriate, email us (below) and we'll remove it.

It says there aren't enough species in my area.

Some places have few recorded observations for some groups, especially fish, herps and mollusks. Try a larger radius, another group, or a nearby town.

Lessons won't load.

KnowKi needs an internet connection to prepare lessons. Check your connection and try again. Your progress is saved on your phone and is never lost when a lesson fails to load.

What does “Native species only” do?

It is on by default and limits lessons to species iNaturalist records as native where each photo was taken. Turn it off to practice introduced species instead. Introduced doesn't always mean invasive.

How do I turn on sounds?

Open Settings → Sounds. Choose a quiet background soundscape (forest birds, a creek or a summer meadow; off by default) and the style of answer sounds, or turn them off. Sounds follow your phone's silent switch.

Why does KnowKi ask for my iNaturalist username but not my password?

KnowKi only reads your public observations to see which of your Fieldbook species you've found. It never posts anything or signs in for you. Disconnect any time in Settings.

How do I delete my data?

Open Settings and tap Delete local progress. Deleting the app also removes everything it stored. There is no account to close.

Can you recommend field guides?

Yes: see field guides for every state. The book links there go to Bookshop.org, which supports independent bookstores; KnowKi earns a commission on purchases through them. The app itself has no store or affiliate links.

How can I support KnowKi?

KnowKi is free, with no ads. If you'd like to help cover the lesson servers, you can buy me a coffee. It's entirely optional and doesn't unlock anything.
