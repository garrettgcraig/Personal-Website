KnowKi Privacy Policy

Effective September 25, 2026 · Updated October 2, 2026 for KnowKi on the web

KnowKi is an iPhone app and a web app (knowki.garrettgcraig.com) made by Garrett Craig. It has no accounts, no advertising or analytics SDKs, and no tracking. This policy explains exactly what the app and its lesson service handle.

On your phone

Your progress stays on your device: which species you've practiced, your answers and review schedule, your Fieldbook, your settings, and your chosen place. You can delete it any time in Settings → Delete local progress, or by deleting the app. iOS device backups may include it, following your iOS settings.

Sent to the KnowKi lesson service

To build a lesson, the app sends the following to the KnowKi lesson service at api.garrettgcraig.com:

  • the center of your chosen place, rounded to about 1 km for searched places or about 11 km for approximate location, and your search radius;
  • the species group and whether you chose native or introduced species;
  • which species are due for review and which of their photos you saw recently, so lessons can repeat the right species with new photos;
  • your answers and requests for a different photo;
  • on a trip, the country, national park or Best Place you chose (its iNaturalist places) instead of a center and radius;
  • when you open a species page, a Meet card or the Daily results, those species, to fetch a sample of where they are recorded and a short introduction from Wikipedia;
  • for the daily game, the edition (U.S. or World), today's date, each answer or running out of time, and whether you swapped the photo;
  • to practice Fieldbook species, those species and which of their photos you saw recently;
  • once you have 10 kin, those species and your place (as above), to show which are most observed nearby this month;
  • if you connect your iNaturalist username, that username and your Fieldbook's species, to check which you've observed. While you type a username, what you type is sent for suggestions.

Your daily scores and shared results stay on your phone. Sharing a result uses the iOS share sheet; nothing is posted for you.

None of this includes your name, an account, a device or advertising identifier, or your precise location. The service does not log IP addresses, answers or locations.

To improve lessons, the service keeps anonymous daily totals: how many lessons were started and finished in each group, how many answers reached each question and how many were correct, how long finished lessons took (only in ranges such as 2–3 minutes), how often photos were swapped, and for the daily game how many were opened and how many answers in each round were right, wrong or out of time. These totals contain no identifier, place or individual answer, so they cannot be traced to you. The timing for a single lesson is deleted with the lesson after 24 hours. To limit abuse it keeps short-lived request counts in memory under a one-way hashed form of your network address; these are never written to disk. Lesson questions and area snapshots are deleted after 24 hours.

An iNaturalist username, what you type while searching for one, and what that account has observed are used only to answer that request: the service never keeps, stores or logs them. KnowKi reads only public observations and never signs in or posts for you. Your username and finds are also stored on your phone; Settings → iNaturalist → Disconnect removes them.

To answer a lesson request, the service asks iNaturalist for species recorded in the rounded area or park. Those requests come from the service, not your phone, and contain no information about you.

The service runs on Fly.io, which carries its network traffic under its own privacy policy.

KnowKi on the web

The web app works like the iPhone app and sends the lesson service the same kinds of information listed above. The differences:

  • Your progress, settings and any connected iNaturalist username are saved in your browser's local storage on that device. They never sync with the iPhone app or other browsers. Settings → Delete local progress, or clearing this site's data in your browser, removes them.
  • Your browser contacts the lesson service at api.garrettgcraig.com directly, so the service sees your network address the same way it does for the iPhone app, and handles it the same way.
  • Searching for a place sends what you type to the lesson service, which looks it up with Photon (an OpenStreetMap search run by komoot) and iNaturalist's place search. Those services see the search text from the lesson service, not from you. The service caches results by search text for up to 30 days, with nothing that identifies you.
  • Use approximate location asks your browser for your location only when you tap it, and rounds it to about 11 km before it is used or sent.
  • Maps show satellite imagery from Esri, loaded by your browser. Esri sees only the map area being shown, not your place or who you are.
  • The site is hosted on Netlify, which serves its pages under its own privacy policy. The web app uses no cookies, analytics or advertising.

Location

Location is optional. The app asks for it only when you tap Use approximate location. It rounds the result to a coarse center and discards the precise fix, and it never tracks you in the background. You can always choose a place by name instead.

Apple services

Searching for a place sends what you type to Apple Maps as you type, so it can suggest towns, parks and addresses. The app asks Apple for the nearest town name for the coarse center of your place, and for the state of places saved by earlier versions. Choosing a trip (a country, national park or Best Place) shows Apple Maps satellite imagery zooming in to it. Photo details show the area where the observation was made, rounded to about 1 km, and species pages show a sample of where the species is recorded, rounded to about 10 km. Map imagery loads from Apple, which sees only the area being shown, not you or your place. Apple handles these under its own privacy policy.

Photos

Quiz photos load through the lesson service. Opening a photo full screen requests a larger copy the same way. Your phone keeps recently viewed photos in its cache for up to a day, so they open quickly. Credit and source links open iNaturalist, Wikipedia or Creative Commons in your browser.

Field guides

The app contains no store or affiliate links. This website has a separate field guides page whose book links are Bookshop.org affiliate links: Bookshop.org credits purchases to KnowKi's Nature Books shop, and KnowKi earns a commission. Bookshop.org handles your visit under its own privacy policy.

Donations

On the U.S. App Store, Settings and About include an optional Buy me a coffee link, and the web app shows it in its sidebar and Settings. It opens Buy Me a Coffee in your browser, which handles any payment under its own privacy policy. The app never learns whether or how much you give, and giving unlocks nothing.

Notifications

After your first lesson you can turn on a daily reminder. It is scheduled on your phone; KnowKi has no push notification server.

Children

KnowKi is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. It has no accounts and collects no names or contact details from anyone.

Changes

If this policy changes, the effective date above will change, and significant changes will be noted in the app's release notes.
