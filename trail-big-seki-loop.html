Big SEKI Loop — July 2026

July 4, 2026 · Sequoia and Kings Canyon National Parks, CA

I hiked the Big SEKI in early July. My iNaturalist record spans July 4–18, 2026: fifteen days of observations tracing a clockwise journey from the Copper Creek side of Kings Canyon, south through the John Muir Trail country, down the Kern, and back through the High Sierra Trail and Elizabeth Pass country. The points also show a Kearsarge Lakes detour.

This is a reconstruction from nature observations rather than a recorded GPS track. The day labels describe travel corridors reconstructed from observations, supplemented by my confirmed overnight stops. The timestamps below are observation windows, not hiking start and finish times, and the last photograph of a day does not establish a campsite.

Day-by-day notes

Day 1 · July 4: Roads End → Granite Lake

The points climb from Roads End through the Copper Creek drainage to Granite Lake. I slept at Granite Lake that night.

Day 2 · July 5: Granite Lake → Granite Pass → Simpson Meadow

The sequence moves north over the Granite Pass corridor, then descends toward the Middle Fork Kings River at Simpson Meadow.

Day 3 · July 6: Simpson Meadow → Middle Fork Kings River

The points follow the Middle Fork upstream. The final reliable record is farther up-canyon toward the Palisade Creek approach.

Day 4 · July 7: Middle Fork Kings → Palisade Creek → Lower Palisade Lake

The sequence turns east along Palisade Creek and climbs the Golden Staircase corridor to the Palisade Lakes. I slept at Lower Palisade Lake that night.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 5 · July 8: Lower Palisade Lake → Mather Pass → Lake Marjorie

The sequence continues south from Lower Palisade Lake over Mather Pass and through Upper Basin toward Lake Marjorie.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 6 · July 9: Lake Marjorie → Pinchot Pass → Twin Lakes vicinity

The records work south from Lake Marjorie across the Pinchot Pass corridor and into the lakes basin below it.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 7 · July 10: Twin Lakes vicinity → Woods Creek → Rae Lakes

The sequence descends to Woods Creek, then climbs toward Rae Lakes. A late-night observation anchors the day in the Rae Lakes area.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 8 · July 11: Rae Lakes → Glen Pass → Kearsarge Lakes

The points head south across the Glen Pass corridor, then east into the Kearsarge Lakes basin. They support a side trip here; they do not establish a resupply or an Onion Valley visit.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 9 · July 12: Kearsarge Lakes → Kearsarge Pass → Wheelbarrow Camp vicinity

The sequence returns west over Kearsarge Pass and works up the Bubbs Creek drainage. I slept around Wheelbarrow Camp, north of Forester Pass.

Day 10 · July 13: Wheelbarrow Camp vicinity → Forester Pass → Junction Meadow

From the Wheelbarrow Camp area, the route continues south over Forester Pass and through the Tyndall Creek and Wallace Creek country. I slept at Junction Meadow that night.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 11 · July 14: Upper Kern → Kern Canyon → Moraine Lake vicinity

The sequence follows the Kern south and then turns west toward the High Sierra Trail and Moraine Lake vicinity.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 12 · July 15: Moraine Lake vicinity → Big Arroyo → Kaweah Gap → Hamilton Lakes

The records move through Big Arroyo and the Kaweah Gap corridor to Hamilton Lakes. The final observation is a rainbow trout at Hamilton Lakes.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 13 · July 16: Hamilton Lakes → Elizabeth Pass approach

The reliable points only cover the morning, ending near the Elizabeth Pass trail approach. The next day begins north of the pass in Deadman Canyon, so the crossing belongs in the gap between these records—not at a confirmed time or campsite.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 14 · July 17: Deadman Canyon → Roaring River → Avalanche Pass corridor

The first record is already north of Elizabeth Pass. The sequence then descends Deadman Canyon toward Roaring River and continues through the Avalanche Pass corridor toward Sphinx Creek.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Day 15 · July 18: Sphinx Creek → lower Bubbs Creek / Roads End approach

The precise records descend toward the lower Bubbs Creek junction. Later, broadly located observations suggest the return toward Roads End, but they are excluded from the plotted reconstruction.

Evidence: first reliable observation · last reliable observation.

Small things along the way

The observations give the route a second scale: whiteleaf manzanita and white bog orchids on the first climb, a mountain cicada near Granite Pass, alpine flowers around Palisade Creek, and fungi in the forests on the way out. A few entries from that record:

  • July 4: white bog orchid on the Copper Creek climb.
  • July 5: mountain cicada along the Granite Pass corridor.
  • July 6: Leichtlin’s mariposa lily above the Middle Fork Kings River.
  • July 9: yellow-bellied marmot in the Upper Basin area.
  • July 15: rainbow trout at Hamilton Lakes.
  • July 18: conifer chicken of the woods in the Sphinx Creek drainage.

How this was reconstructed

The map uses 594 public observations from my iNaturalist account, grouped by the date they were observed in Pacific time. Only unobscured points in the trip area with reported location uncertainty of 500 meters or less are plotted. Records with missing coordinates, unspecified accuracy, or larger uncertainty remain on iNaturalist but do not anchor the itinerary. The published coordinates have been rounded to six decimal places.

The Big SEKI route description by Amy and James at Doing Miles provides context for the named trail corridors. Their standard loop is about 155 miles; that is a reference distance, not a measurement of my hike or its Kearsarge detour. These scattered observations do not establish daily trail mileage or elevation gain. In particular, travel after the July 16 morning records and the exact July 18 finish remain estimates.
