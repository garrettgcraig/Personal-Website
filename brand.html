01 · Essence

Four guiding words

curiousLead with the question. Name the species, the place, the thing you didn't expect.
groundedEvidence before adjectives. Real data, real places, honest limits.
clearOne idea per screen, plain words, generous space. Maps and charts that read in seconds.
warmSunlit paper and ochre, not chrome and neon. Friendly, unhurried, a little funny.

Personality

A field scientist who also ships software: careful with claims, generous with explanation, happiest outdoors. The site should feel clean, warm, and calm, like a well-kept field notebook left open on a sunny table.

Inspiration

  • Georgia O'Keeffe: high-desert sky, desert ochres, sage, soft flower forms. The palette.
  • Henri Cartier-Bresson: geometry, black and white, the decisive moment. The layout and photo edit.
  • Michael Pollan: the garden as a lens on big systems, told as narrative nonfiction. The writing.
  • Robin Wall Kimmerer: sweetgrass, moss, reciprocity; attention as a gift. The ethic.

04 · Signature graphic

Layered ridgelines

Four soft ridges receding into a high-desert sky, like an O'Keeffe hill seen as a contour map. Use as a section divider, page footer band, or social-card background. Keep it wide and low; never stack text on the darkest ridge.

05 · Mark

The Ridgeline G

An open G whose crossbar is a mountain ridge: the monogram and the landscape in one stroke. Paper on ink is primary; ink on paper is the reverse. Keep clear space of one stroke width on all sides and don't recolor the ridge.

08 · Voice

How it sounds

First person, plain American English, specific nouns. Write like a field note to a smart friend.

Do

  • Name the thing: "Eastern newt in Pisgah National Forest," not "a cool salamander."
  • Lead with what it does: "Maps where roads cut wildlife corridors."
  • Give numbers with units and sources.
  • Say what you don't know yet.
  • Let a little humor in, especially off the clock.

Don't

  • "Cutting-edge," "leveraging," "innovative solutions," "passionate about."
  • Claims a figure or a paper can't back up.
  • Jargon without a gloss (IPLC → Indigenous Peoples and local communities).
  • Walls of text; break it up with a map, a photo, or a number.
  • Exclamation points in professional copy.
09 · Photography

Photo style

Natural light, honest color, and a clear subject. Wait for the decisive moment: the animal's glance, the light breaking on a ridge. Favor strong geometry (horizons, diagonals, negative space) over busy frames. Black and white is welcome when shape is the story.

  • No heavy HDR, oversaturation, or vignettes.
  • Keep crops close to the original aspect ratio; let images breathe on paper.
  • Caption as Title, then species or place and year.
  • Always write descriptive alt text.
